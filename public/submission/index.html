AgroOrbit Nexus
From Space to Soil

“In Virú, water arrives by canal and costs money. AgroOrbit shows how much water each rotation needs, what it does for the soil, and how it would have handled El Niño, before you plant.”

Compare crop rotations by water, soil and risk, before you plant.

Built for agricultural extension agents who review rotation options with farmers. That is why it compares and explains instead of recommending.

Exploration, not a recommendation · No regional agronomist validation yet

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data. First pilot: Virú, La Libertad, Peru (illustrative crop history, real NASA climate).

1 / 7

In a desert valley, the question is canal water, not rain.

  • Virú is irrigated by the Chavimochic system, which supplies the Chao, Virú and Moche valleys with water drawn from the Santa river (gob.pe · Chavimochic, gob.pe · Santa river reserve).
  • The demo field repeats the same crop: 3 maize campaigns in a row, 1 crop family.
  • Model rainfall is not a reliable guide on the coastal desert, so it is shown with less weight and never used to rank options.

So AgroOrbit answers: how many m³ per hectare would each rotation need, and what would it do for the soil?

Repeated cropping can motivate a review of pests and nutrition; it does not prove damage. No damage is claimed for this field.

13,120A · maize → maize → maize (m³/ha)
12,720B · maize → bean → maize (m³/ha)
11,730C · maize → sorghum → bean (m³/ha)

Bottom line: C needs ~1,390 m³/ha less water than A (139 mm), uses 2 crop families and breaks the maize cycle.

Crop water demand ETc = Kc × ET0 (FAO-56: Hargreaves ET0, eq. 52, from NASA POWER daily temperatures; generic Kc, tables 11–12), over a common 540-day horizon. 1 mm = 10 m³/ha. Optional user tariff (S/ per m³) turns m³ into soles; no default tariff is ever used. Demand of a healthy crop, not a validated irrigation requirement.

Farmer priorities re-order the options live.

Three sliders (0–100): Water (lower FAO-56 demand), Soil health, Diversity / pest-cycle break. Formula on screen: score = Σ wᵢ·sᵢ / Σ wᵢ, each criterion normalised 0–1 across the options shown.

Virú demo, real computed scores. Every option gets a numeric “Why it ranks here” line.

When water matters most, C leads; when soil matters most, B leads because it leaves the soil uncropped fewer days. The extension agent and the farmer decide; the tool shows the trade-off.

6 / 7

Open, reproducible, and honest about limits.

  • Try it: open the app → EN → “Try the Virú demo”. The demo keeps working offline from a stored, unmodified NASA POWER response.
  • Global: NASA POWER climate for any point on Earth; agronomic comparisons only where a regional profile exists. No recommendations anywhere.
  • Verified: unit and browser tests (desktop, mobile, tablet) with automated accessibility checks; every number on these slides comes from the app’s computations.
  • Limits: illustrative crop history; generic FAO-56 Kc; Hargreaves ET0; no field measurements, yields, profits or savings; no regional agronomist validation yet.
  • AI disclosure: AI coding assistants helped with research, programming, testing and documentation; the team reviewed the results. The app itself uses explicit, documented rules: no trained model, no runtime AI.
